Galactic Neutral Hydrogen Structures Spectroscopy and Kinematics: Designing a Radio Telescope for 21 cm Emission

Apr 2024 – Oct 2024

The radio astronomy dish (left) on my rooftop tower.

I designed and built a radio telescope on my roof that uses a dish, hardware and software to detect 21 cm emissions (f21 = 1420.405 MHz) and determine the distribution and kinematics of neutral hydrogen in the Milky Way. I analyzed the data and summarized the results of my research in a 28 page paper published on arXiv. One of my project goals was to create an affordable tech stack and template that students and hobbyists can follow to conduct this type of research.

Using this system, I am able to

  • measure the kinematics (movement) of our galaxy
  • map the distribution of hydrogen gas
  • derive velocity information from this data using Python scripts

System components

  • 1 meter parabolic dish with beam width of 14º
  • low noise amplifiers, bandpass filters, software defined radio and a computer; the setup also includes various RFI mitigation techniques

Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence (SETI) Institute

Jul 2025

SETI reached out to me after reading my paper on 21 cm galactic kinematics. At SETI I learned about the analog and digital signal processing chains and performed maintenance on the cryogenically cooled feeds, the antenna control systems, fiber optics and converters, GPU correlators and beamformers.

I also assisted in conducting multiple surveys with the telescope as a whole, successfully imaging quasar 3C 147, over 3 billion light years away. I additionally conducted surveys of Mars and monitored the transmissions of Mars orbiters. Using JPL ephemeris data and doppler shift calculations, I was able to successfully validate the velocity of the orbiters.

Galileo Project — Passive Radar

Nov 2025 – Present · Principal RF engineer

Passive radar station; EO/IR station; radar track in 3D and 2D.

I am the principal RF engineer at the Galileo Project where I am leading the development of a multistatic passive radar system to identify and track airborne objects. The Galileo Project is a research lab at Harvard led by Professor Avi Loeb and is attempting to design a new sensor suite to track and catalog UAP (unidentified anomalous phenomena).

My work has consisted of designing antenna systems and data acquisition pipelines to determine bistatic ranges of airborne objects using passive radar techniques. I am also working to develop sensor fusion algorithms that combine measurements from EO, IR, radar and acoustic sensors. I recently designed an innovative passive radar system that I will install on the top of the Las Vegas Sphere later this year.

Phoenix Fire Labs

Nov 2025 – Present · Co-founder

After losing my home, neighborhood and community to the Palisades Fire in January 2025, I decided to co-found Phoenix Fire Labs, a company focused on using technology to enhance strategic firefighting. Our long-term goal is to build a real-time “digital twin” of wildfires: a system in which the fire exists as comprehensively in the digital domain as it does in the physical world, enabling better data analysis, stronger strategic decision-making, and more effective firefighting.

For now, we are focused on developing an AI transcription tool that automatically transcribes firefighters’ radio transmissions, extracts location data and operational descriptions, and plots them onto a shared geospatial map. The result is a clearer common operating picture and greater situational awareness, both of which were shortcomings during the Palisades Fire.

Antenna Tower

July 2022 – Present

I designed and constructed an antenna tower on my roof that houses antennas and servers. The tower tracks aircraft, receives and demodulates data directly from satellites, generates income, and provides encrypted communications. All these projects involve antennas and signal processing, which are vital in instrumentation and astronomy. My work in these areas focussed my interest in instrumentation as it synthesizes my interests in technology and engineering with broader questions about the universe. The tower includes:

  • ADS-B node, a specialized antenna setup optimized for receiving data from aircraft within a 100 nm radius of my house. The data includes things like aircraft position, speed, and squawk code, and the data is provided to Air Traffic Control.
  • Two satellite communication antennas: a NOAA V-dipole made from copper pipe and a dish, that both use multiple LNAs and BPFs to enhance signal reception. The V-dipole receives images of the earth multiple times a day as NOAA satellites pass overhead. The satellites transmit images via automatic picture transmission (APT), and my antenna picks up the signal, demodulates it and stores the picture on a server at my house. The dish antenna is pointed at a geostationary satellite (GOES-16) located over 38,000 km away. The data stream is demodulated and the resulting images show ⅓ of the earth at a given time.
  • Meshtastic LoRaWAN node, part of a larger network infrastructure project I am working on (e.g. I have a separate node at a friend’s house and am working with my school to install a node there) that provides encrypted communications in the larger LA area. The nodes are solar powered and the radio protocol does not rely on the internet (users on the network will be able to text even in a power outage).
  • Helium node, which works to provide IoT coverage for the larger Los Angeles area. The node mostly handles packet transfers from things like Bird scooters and other IoT devices, and I am paid for this.
